## Title (Title Case; the PDF now carries the same)

No Speech Articulator Robustly Favours Retroauricular Electrodes Over Canonical Jaw Sites: A Volume-Conductor Model With Orientation and Electrode-Count Controls

## Authors

Carl Vincent Ladres Kho, Minerva University, San Francisco, California, United States, kho@uni.minerva.edu (already linked). ORCID: 0000-0003-3131-7214. DBLP: N/A.

## Submission Abstract (147 words; identical to the PDF)

Ear-worn biopotential devices are designed around an uncomputed coupling: how strongly each speech articulator reaches electrodes on the jaw and around the ear. We compute it by reciprocity on MIDA, a head model with 116 labelled compartments at 500 µm, and test it against three spurious sources: source orientation, electrode count, and anatomical detail. Twenty-two electrode positions across jaw, retroauricular and cEEGrid montages are compared against ten segmented articulators. Five articulators favour the jaw montage at every orientation and every electrode subsample; none robustly favours the retroauricular montage. The strongest ear-leaning candidate, temporalis, reaches −2.57 dB under a uniform sweep but −1.15 dB, interval [−1.45, +5.46], under its fibre field. For device design the result is one-sided: a retroauricular montage loses lip and chin activity and reliably buys none back. Placement remains a lever: a four-site cluster chosen anatomically beats arbitrary placement by up to 1.07 dB.
