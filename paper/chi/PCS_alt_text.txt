Figure 1: "Three views (lateral, frontal, axial) of the MIDA head model with the face removed anterior of the eyes. The ten segmented articulator muscles are highlighted in colour and all 22 electrode positions are marked as dots: the canonical jaw montage along the jaw and chin, a retroauricular cluster behind the ear, and a cEEGrid C-shaped path around the ear."
Figure 2: "A heat-map matrix of 22 electrode sites (rows) by 10 articulator muscles (columns). Each cell is the median lead field in that muscle in dB relative to the muscle's best jaw site, which is ringed at 0 dB. Red cells are attenuation relative to the best jaw site, blue cells exceed it; boxed cells mark every site and muscle pair where a retroauricular electrode beats every jaw electrode. The colour scale runs from -28 dB to +4 dB and is not symmetric."
Figure 3: "A horizontal bar chart with one bar per articulator: the difference in dB between the best jaw site and the best retroauricular site. Positive bars (jaw advantage) reach +20.90 dB; negative bars (ear advantage) reach only -3.31 dB. A shaded band around zero marks the electrode-meshing floor of 0.27 dB, widening to its 0.65 dB upper confidence bound."
Figure 4: "A matrix of 22 sites by 10 muscles showing the change in lead field between the isotropic and anisotropic conductivity conditions, in dB. Only two muscles, sternocleidomastoid and medial pterygoid, received a fibre tensor and show non-zero cells; the other eight rows are labelled 'not applied'."
Figure 5: "A scatter plot of lead field in dB (vertical) against straight-line electrode-to-muscle distance in millimetres (horizontal), one point per electrode and muscle pair, with jaw and retroauricular sites in two colours. Two fitted lines over the shared 3 to 87 mm range show the retroauricular sites falling off at -0.158 dB per mm against the jaw's -0.045 dB per mm."
Figure 6: "A scatter plot with seven points, one per muscle with a layer profile: the adipose fraction of the muscle-to-skin path (horizontal) against the material share of that muscle's jaw-versus-ear gap (vertical). The relationship is negative (Spearman rho -0.955); points are marked by whether the muscle's gap resolves to the jaw or shows no resolvable preference. No trend line is drawn."
Figure 7: "A paired horizontal bar chart, one row per muscle, splitting each muscle's jaw-versus-ear gap into a geometric term (up to 19.1 dB, always toward the jaw) and a material term (never above 2.9 dB, pointing toward the jaw for five muscles and toward the ear for five)."
Figure 8: "Left: a colour map on an oblique anatomical plane through the head containing the mental (jaw) electrode, the mastoid (ear) electrode and the orbicularis oris centroid, showing the ratio of the two electrodes' sensitivity fields; red regions favour the jaw electrode, blue favour the ear electrode, and a heavy contour marks where they are equal. Right: median field magnitude against distance from the injecting electrode on log axes, with the ten muscle compartments marked at their own median distances; both electrodes fall on a single decay line (Spearman rho -0.961, slope -1.42)."
Figure 9: "Two anatomical slices, sagittal and coronal, showing the magnitude of the sensitivity field of the retroauricular montage through the pooled general-muscle compartment that contains the suprahyoid muscles. The mastoid notch, the hyoid greater horn and the corridor between them are outlined over the field."
Figure 10: "Left: the temporalis jaw-versus-ear gap at four successive analysis stages, drawn as points with matched-count intervals: field magnitude against the best of fourteen ear sites, projection onto source orientation, matched electrode counts, and the fibre field derived from the label volume; only the last interval spans zero. Right: a histogram of the gap over all 1,001 possible four-site retroauricular montages, with dotted lines at the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles; 28.6 percent of montages sit exactly at the lower bound."
Figure 11: "A horizontal bar chart, one bar per muscle, spanning the envelope of the jaw-versus-ear gap over all five admissible jaw subsets at four electrodes each, coloured by verdict, with the orientation-agreement range shown at the right. Five bars sit entirely on the jaw side; two cross zero and are marked unstable; temporalis sits on the ear side on the statistic-A basis, and a second black rule beneath it shows the same comparison under the derived fibre field, which spans zero."
